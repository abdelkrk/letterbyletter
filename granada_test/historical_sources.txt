GRANADA PROJECT — HISTORICAL SOURCE LOG
Compiled via WebSearch during Phase C research. Each entry lists the claim it
supports and the source(s) consulted. Wikipedia/tertiary sources were used to
orient research and cross-checked against more specific/academic material where
available; no single blog was relied on as sole authority for a contested claim.

CLAIM: Treaty/Capitulations of Granada signed 25 November 1491 at Santa Fe;
67 articles; guarantees of religion, mosques, property, own law, 3-year tax
exemption, right to emigrate to North Africa with possessions.

CLAIM: Formal surrender/transfer of the city and the Alhambra took place
2 January 1492; Boabdil (Muhammad XII / Abu Abdallah) handed the keys to
Ferdinand II of Aragon and Isabella I of Castile; handover was deliberately
quiet/choreographed given a city stressed by siege and refugees.
SOURCES:
- "Treaty of Granada (1491)" — Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Granada_(1491)

CLAIM: Francisco Jiménez de Cisneros arrived in Granada in summer 1499 and
adopted a coercive conversion policy: imprisoning uncooperative Muslims
(especially notables) until they converted; reported ~3,000 conversions in a
single day by December 1499; targeted "elches" (former Christians converted
to Islam) for forced reversion.
SOURCES:
- "Francisco Jiménez de Cisneros" — Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Jim%C3%A9nez_de_Cisneros

  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebellion_of_the_Alpujarras_(1499%E2%80%931501)

in the Plaza de Bibarrambla, Granada; contemporary sources put the minimum
figure at over 5,000 volumes; medical texts were reportedly excluded.

CLAIM: 12 February 1502 Pragmatic issued for the Crown of Castile ordering
conversion or expulsion of all Mudéjares; adult Muslims (males 14+, females
12+) had until end of April 1502 to convert or leave via the single
designated port of Biscay, without gold or silver; most converted, becoming
known as "Moriscos."
SOURCES:
- "Forced conversions of Muslims in Spain" — Wikipedia,
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forced_conversions_of_Muslims_in_Spain
- "February 12, 1502. The Muslim Conversion or Expulsion" — VCoins Community,
  https://social.vcoins.com/twih/february-12-1502-muslim-conversion-expulsion/
NOTE: This 1502 Castilian decree is broader than Granada specifically; used
here only as general background on the era's conversion pressure, not
narrated as a specific Granada-only event in the script.

CLAIM: War/Rebellion of the Alpujarras (1568-1571): began Christmas Eve 1568
in Granada city, then spread to the Alpujarras; rebels proclaimed Aben Humeya
(Fernando de Valor, claiming Umayyad descent) as king; rebel forces grew to
roughly 25,000; Aben Humeya was killed (strangled) in an internal coup on
20 October 1569; succeeded by Aben Aboo; suppressed by March/May 1571 with
the arrival of ~20,000 royal troops under John of Austria.
SOURCES:
- "Rebellion of the Alpujarras (1568-1571)" — Wikipedia,
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebellion_of_the_Alpujarras_(1568%E2%80%931571)
- "Aben Humeya" — Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ab%C3%A9n_Humeya
- "Aben Humeya: Morisco King of the 1568 Alpujarras Revolt" — ExploreGranada,
  https://www.exploregranada.org/articles/aben-humeya-morisco-rebellion-alpujarras-1568

CLAIM: Philip II ordered mass deportation of the Granadan Morisco population
(~80,000 people) into Old and New Castile before the rebellion was fully
suppressed, dispersing them in small groups (e.g., Córdoba, Toledo, León,
Seville) to prevent organized resistance, separating families; the
Alpujarras region was then repopulated by Christian settlers, underfunded
and far below the displaced population.
SOURCES:
- "Rebellion of the Alpujarras (1568-1571)" — Wikipedia
- "The Alpujarras Villages: Morisco Refuge After 1492" — ExploreGranada,
  https://www.exploregranada.org/articles/alpujarras-morisco-villages-history
NOTE: the ~80,000 figure and dispersal-city list are treated in the script as
approximate/general ("عشرات الآلاف") rather than an exact stated number, per
project instructions not to over-specify unresearched precision.

CLAIM: Philip III signed the Edict of Expulsion on 9 April 1609; expulsion
began in the Kingdom of Valencia in September 1609 (public announcement
22 September; departures from ports such as Dénia from 2 October); extended
via subsequent decrees to Aragon (1610), Castile (1610-ish, executed over
~1611-1614 per some sources), and Andalusia (1610-1614); total expelled
estimated around 300,000, with Valencia's ~120,000 the hardest hit single
region.
SOURCES:
- "The expulsion of the Moriscos from Spain in 1609-1614: the destruction of
  an Islamic periphery" — Journal of Global History / Cambridge Core (academic),
  https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-global-history/article/abs/expulsion-of-the-moriscos-from-spain-in-16091614-the-destruction-of-an-islamic-periphery/CA674D527734A60621865F2A99FB60C7
- "Philip III of Spain Orders the Expulsion of the Moriscos, September 22,
  1609" — VCoins Community, https://social.vcoins.com/twih/philip-iii-of-spain-orders-the-expulsion-of-the-moriscos-september-22-1609-r448/
- "The Royal Edict of Expulsion (1609) and the Last Andalusi Muslims
  (Moriscos) of Spain" — Ballandalus, https://ballandalus.wordpress.com/2015/06/29/the-royal-edict-of-expulsion-1609-and-the-last-andalusi-muslims-moriscos-of-spain/
NOTE: Castile's exact expulsion window is reported inconsistently across
sources (one cites 1610, another 1611-1614); the script deliberately uses
the general phrase "على مدى السنوات التالية" (over the following years)
rather than committing to a single precise Castilian date, and this should
be tightened further with an academic monograph (e.g., Lapeyre, or the
Bernabé-Pons overview cited below) before any final broadcast-grade claim.

GENERAL CAVEATS FOR FINAL FACT-CHECK PASS BEFORE TTS:
1. The Castile-specific expulsion chronology (as applied to Diego's fictional
   family) should be double-checked against the Bernabé-Pons (EGO) academic
   overview or an equivalent monograph before finalizing exact wording — this
   script currently uses only general phrasing ("امتد تدريجياً... على مدى
   السنوات التالية") to avoid asserting a single incorrect date.
2. The 5,000-volume book-burning figure is presented in the script as the
   documented minimum, not a total; phrasing avoids "all Arabic books were
   destroyed."
3. No invented dialogue or fabricated quotations from Muhammad XII,
   Ferdinand, Isabella, Cisneros, or Núñez Muley appear anywhere in the
   script. All historical figures are described only in third-person
   narration of documented actions.
4. Yusuf, Miguel, and Diego are explicitly flagged as fictional narrative
   devices at the top of voiceover_script.txt and introduced with "لنتخيل"
   ("let us imagine") at each generational entry point, per project
   requirements.
